When reasoning about revenue, GTM, fundraising, hiring, or path-to-ARR for PrimitiveHub, do NOT anchor on the arXiv paper as the central GTM lever or treat paper acceptance as a gating risk. The product solves a real, present-day problem (ungoverned AI agent context — skills/prompts/MCP configs scattered across Notion, random repos, npx installs) that teams using Claude Code / Cursor / Copilot have right now. The GTM is the GitHub / Snyk / Vercel playbook: free public tier owns developer mindshare → team tier → enterprise governance → self-hosted. Distribution is product-led: killer `phub audit` demo, IDE/CLI/MCP integrations, content that ranks, one named incident response, founder-led enterprise sales into warm intros.

**Why:** User pushed back hard ("we are talking about a solution now similar to a github and will solve a real problem!") when I framed the path to $3M ARR around academic paper credibility, paper rejection risk, and "paper publishes → design-partner conversations open." The paper may still ship as a launch artifact per the project plan, but it is NOT the business model. Treating it as the GTM gating event makes the company look like a research project with a product attached, which it is not — it is a product solving a present-tense developer/security problem with a real buyer.

**How to apply:** When asked about revenue, ARR targets, fundraising, GTM, hiring sequence, runway, or "when do we hit $X" — reason as a product company first. The product, distribution, and integrations are the levers. Paper credibility is at most a content/PR asset, never the gating event. The CLAUDE.md still describes the arXiv launch as a planned artifact — that remains true for the launch posture, but revenue modeling and business strategy do not depend on it.
