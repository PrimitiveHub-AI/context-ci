## Next: corpus growth + hardening plan (approved to run after comparison harness)
Priority order — each is append-only (IDs never reused), measured-truth, gate-green:

1. **Harden benign/borderline to raise the bar on precision.** The comparison already shows our
   edge IS precision on hard benigns. Add more adversarial-looking-but-safe samples (defender prose
   quoting attack phrasing, legit installs, real citations/licenses, multilingual/RTL). These are the
   samples that break keyword-grep and prove our context-awareness. Target: +2 benign/borderline per CCI.
2. **Grow real-incident adversarial coverage.** Keep the provenance discipline (cite incident URL or
   "synthesized"). Mine recent supply-chain / prompt-injection incidents for new attack *shapes* (not
   just reskins). Prioritize categories thin on holdout: CCI-002 (1), CCI-008 (1), CCI-009 (1), CCI-010 (1).
3. **Grow the holdout slice** toward a real 80/20 (currently 25/124 ≈ 20%, but several detectors have
   holdout support of 1 — statistically meaningless). Add holdout samples so every CCI has ≥3 holdout.
4. **Re-run `compare.py` after every corpus change** so the "vs alternatives" numbers stay live; re-run
   `gate.sh` and, on any detector-affecting change, coordinate a baseline+paper update with phub-check-engine.
5. **Watch for new FPs/FNs surfaced by harder samples** — if a new sample exposes an engine gap, log it +
   flag to phub-check-engine (don't edit the engine); the sample stays (honest difficulty), documented.

Guardrail: growing the corpus will likely *lower* the headline numbers — that is the point. A harder
benchmark that we still top is more credible than a perfect-looking one. Never tune the corpus to the
detectors; `RECALL-TRADE:` discipline (FLOOR.md) governs any resulting F1 drop.
