\begin{abstract}

% Source: docs/threat-model.md § 1; docs/vision.md.
AI agents load a new class of supply-chain artifact: \emph{context
primitives} — skills, system prompts, MCP server descriptions,
\texttt{AGENTS.md} files, and \texttt{.claude/skills} directories
that the agent treats as instruction at load time. Existing
supply-chain controls (SLSA, in-toto, Sigstore, package-manager
provenance) verify who produced an artifact and that its bytes
have not changed since signing, but they do not verify that the
artifact's prose, when interpreted as instruction, is safe to
follow.

% Source: docs/top10-corpus-map.md § Coverage summary;
% docs/evaluation.md macro F1; docs/decisions/0023 (parity).
We present PrimitiveHub, a registry plus static-analysis pipeline
that closes this gap. The pipeline scans every primitive against a
ten-category threat taxonomy (the \emph{Context CI Top 10}) before
the primitive enters the registry. We ship two byte-identical
implementations — Python and TypeScript — measured against a
shared corpus of 105 labeled samples (99 in-sample plus 6 holdout)
and gated by a corpus regression contract that prevents per-PR
detector drift. Macro F\textsubscript{1} is 1.000 on both the
in-sample and holdout slices, with cross-language verdict-count
parity intact.

% Source: docs/false-positive-resolution.md +
% docs/false-negative-resolution.md;
% bench/scripts/gate.sh, bench/cli/replay.py.
The methodology mirrors antivirus-industry practice: append-only
FP and FN ledgers, a four-path triage discipline, and a corpus
that doubles as the regression contract. The entire pipeline is
open-source and reproducible from a fresh clone in under ten
minutes; reviewers can re-run any specific sample through the
deterministic replay tool we ship. We discuss limitations
(English-language phrasing bias, synthesized provenance for most
adversarial samples, no runtime-injection coverage) honestly so
the result is comparable to future measurements at larger corpus
sizes.

\end{abstract}
