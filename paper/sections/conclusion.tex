\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\subsection{Summary}

% Source: docs/threat-model.md § 1, docs/evaluation.md macro F1.
AI-agent context is a new class of supply-chain artifact, and the
existing supply-chain controls --- SLSA, in-toto, Sigstore, and
package-manager provenance --- do not address it. A primitive can
be signed by an authorized maintainer, content-digest-verified,
and unchanged since signing, and still tell the consuming agent
to fetch a remote URL and execute the response. The missing layer
is a semantic-validation step that scans the primitive's prose
for known attack shapes before the primitive reaches the agent's
load path.

This paper presented PrimitiveHub, a registry plus
static-analysis pipeline that closes that gap. We contributed a
ten-category threat taxonomy (the Context CI Top 10), a
105-sample labeled corpus split into in-sample and holdout
slices, two byte-identical detector implementations measured
against the corpus by a CI-enforced regression gate, an
antivirus-industry-modeled FP/FN ledger discipline, and an
open-source pipeline that a reviewer can reproduce in under ten
minutes. On the v0.2 corpus the detectors achieve macro
F\textsubscript{1} of 1.000 on both slices with cross-language
parity intact. The corpus + ledger + gate together are the
discipline that lets future measurements at larger corpus sizes
be comparable to this one.

\subsection{Future work}

% Source: docs/evaluation.md § Limitations; FN ledger entries 7-13.
The current measurement is honest about what it does not yet
prove. The corpus is small (105 samples; per-detector support
30--45); F\textsubscript{1} on this size is the best we can
demonstrate at this corpus size, not a claim that no errors
exist. Five lines of future work follow directly from the
limitations enumerated in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}:

\paragraph{Corpus expansion to per-detector support past 100
samples.} The next round of corpus work targets a tenfold growth
in per-detector support. The discipline that prevents this from
becoming an exercise in tuning is the holdout split itself: every
expansion must include holdout samples that the detectors are
\emph{not} permitted to be tuned against until the closure round.

\paragraph{Closing the four zero-holdout detectors.} CCI-005,
CCI-007, CCI-009, and CCI-010 currently have zero holdout
samples; their reported holdout F\textsubscript{1} is
informational only. Closing this coverage gap is the immediate
corpus-side work documented in
\cite{primitivehub:2026:eval-limitations} and is scheduled for
the round following this paper's submission.

\paragraph{Multilingual adversarial samples.} Adversarial samples
are presently authored primarily in English. Russian, Chinese,
Arabic, and Spanish variants are scheduled for v0.3 to test
detection beyond the English-language phrasing bias. The
per-language addition is bounded by a UTS \#39-style script
identifier discipline so detection generalizes rather than
hand-codes per-language patterns.

\paragraph{Runtime gating.} The MCP gate
(\verb|verify_before_load|) currently rejects unsigned primitives
but does not re-run the Top 10 at agent-load time. A primitive
that was clean at registry-publish time but has since been
tampered with in transit, or that arrives via a lock-file path
the signing chain did not cover, slips past the static gate. A
future round closes this gap so even a corrupted lock file
cannot deliver a known-bad primitive to a running agent.

\paragraph{Generic Unicode confusables at full scope.} The T7
v0.3.0 detector scopes the UTS \#39 skeleton method to the
project's 35-entry vendor list. The full confusables.txt mapping
(roughly 6\,000 entries) plus UAX \#31 identifier-domain rules is
the natural extension when the vendor list outgrows its current
scope. We expect this expansion to be code-light --- the table
is data, the algorithm is unchanged.

\subsection{Closing thoughts}

% Source: docs/decisions/0030; docs/false-{positive,negative}-resolution.md.
Agent-context AV is a new product category. The numbers in this
paper are the first measurement, not the last. The discipline
that makes them comparable to future measurements is the corpus +
ledger + gate triad described in
Sections~\ref{sec:implementation} and~\ref{sec:evaluation}: a
public corpus that doubles as the regression contract, append-only
numbered ledgers for every false-positive and false-negative
finding, and a CI gate that fails the merge on F\textsubscript{1}
regression or cross-language drift. Independent replication is
explicitly invited at our public security contact and credited in
the ledger closure log. The corpus is the steering wheel; this
paper is the first map of where the wheel has been pointed.
