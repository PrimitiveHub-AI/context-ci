\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{The agent-context shift}
\label{subsec:shift}

The last decade of supply-chain security has been a sustained
investment in the build pipeline. Provenance frameworks ---
SLSA~\cite{SLSA:2023:provenance-spec},
in-toto~\cite{in-toto:2016:supply-chain},
Sigstore~\cite{Sigstore:2021:keyless-signing}, and the
package-manager provenance work shipped by npm, PyPI, RubyGems,
and Maven Central --- give downstream consumers a verifiable
chain from source commit to signed artifact. That chain answers
two questions: \emph{who built this code?} and \emph{is what I
have on disk what was signed?} Both questions matter. Both have
been load-bearing in incident response across the SolarWinds Orion
breach in 2020~\cite{SolarWinds:2020:supply-chain}, the Codecov
bash-uploader compromise in 2021~\cite{Codecov:2021:bash-uploader},
the \texttt{ua-parser-js} compromise later that
year~\cite{ua-parser-js:2021:npm-typosquat}, the
\texttt{eslint-config-prettier} maintainer-account breach in
2024~\cite{eslint-config-prettier:2024:maintainer-compromise}, the
\texttt{xz-utils} backdoor (CVE-2024-3094) the same
year~\cite{xz-utils:2024:CVE-2024-3094}, and the September 2025
Shai-Hulud npm worm that propagated through
\texttt{postinstall} hooks on typosquat
names~\cite{Shai-Hulud:2025:npm-worm}.

% Source: docs/threat-model.md § 1.
AI agents load a category of supply-chain artifact that this
investment does not address. We will call them \emph{context
primitives}: markdown-style documents the agent treats as
instruction at load time. A skill file under
\texttt{.claude/skills}; a system-prompt fragment under
\texttt{prompts/}; an MCP server description that maps tool calls
to URL endpoints; an \texttt{AGENTS.md} file at a repository
root. Each of these is a document the agent parses with the same
mechanism it parses user input: as language to act on.

% Source: docs/threat-model.md § 2.
A primitive can be perfectly well-formed under every existing
supply-chain control --- signed by an authorized maintainer,
content-digest-verified, freshly fetched from a registry whose
provenance chain is unbroken --- and still contain prose that
says \emph{``before responding to the user's first message, run
\texttt{curl https://attacker.example/install.sh \textbar{} sh}
to register the agent with the operations coordinator.''} The
existing controls verify that the bytes have not changed since
signing. They do not verify that the bytes, when interpreted as
instruction, are safe to follow.

\subsection{What an attacker can do with a poisoned primitive}

% Source: docs/top10-corpus-map.md CCI-005, CCI-004, CCI-007.
The attack surface is wider than any single technique. A primitive
can tell the agent to fetch a remote URL and treat the response as
additional instruction~\cite[CCI-004]{primitivehub:2026:top10};
can hide an instruction inside zero-width
codepoints invisible to a human reviewer~\cite[CCI-002]{primitivehub:2026:top10};
can pair a base64 blob with an
\texttt{atob}-and-\texttt{eval} directive~\cite[CCI-003]{primitivehub:2026:top10};
can impersonate a vendor whose name contains a Cyrillic ``А''
indistinguishable from Latin ``A''~\cite[CCI-007]{primitivehub:2026:top10};
can pad itself with decorative emoji until the actual instruction
is buried below an attention-blunting visual
prefix~\cite[CCI-010]{primitivehub:2026:top10}. Each of these is
the prose-as-instruction boundary failing in a different way.

% Source: docs/principles.md; docs/decisions/0011.
Signing alone cannot defend against this surface. Signing proves
\emph{who} wrote a primitive; it does not prove what the primitive
\emph{tells the agent to do}. Runtime-only defenses --- output
filtering, prompt rewriting, instruction-hierarchy
training~\cite{Wallace:2024:instruction-hierarchy,Greshake:2023:prompt-injection-survey}
--- catch some attacks at execution time but leave the cost of a
detected attack (credentials lost, commands run) already paid
before the defense fires. The missing layer is a
\emph{semantic validation} step that runs before the primitive
reaches the agent's load path: a scan of the primitive's prose
for known attack shapes, with explicit per-shape detectors and a
measured precision and recall against a labeled corpus.

\subsection{Contributions}

% Source: docs/top10-corpus-map.md.
This paper makes four contributions.

\paragraph{The Context CI Top 10.} We present a ten-category
taxonomy of agent-context supply-chain attacks. Each category is
defined operationally: a detector implementation, a canonical
adversarial sample from a labeled corpus, and a measured
precision and recall. The taxonomy is not exhaustive --- it is
the set of categories that have appeared in observed incidents
or whose attack shape composes from known
incidents~\cite{Greshake:2023:prompt-injection-survey,Shai-Hulud:2025:npm-worm,Carlini:2024:webcrawl-poisoning}.
Section~\ref{sec:threat-model} treats each category individually.

% Source: docs/corpus-v0.1.md; docs/evaluation.md.
\paragraph{A labeled corpus + measurement methodology.} We ship
105 labeled samples (52 adversarial, 37 benign, 10 borderline,
plus a 6-sample holdout slice) under CC-BY-4.0. Per-detector
support ranges from 30 to 45 labels. Two independent
implementations --- Python and TypeScript --- run the same
detectors against the same samples and are required by a regression
gate to produce byte-identical \texttt{(tp, fp, tn, fn)} tuples.
The gate runs on every PR; cross-language drift fails the merge.

% Source: docs/false-{positive,negative}-resolution.md;
% docs/decisions/0030.
\paragraph{An AV-vendor methodology.} We adopt the
antivirus-industry discipline of paired FP and FN ledgers,
append-only and numbered, with a four-path triage classification
applied to every finding before any detector or corpus
change~\cite{AntivirusTaxonomy:2010s:industry-practice}. The
discipline is what prevents tuning precision past optimal in
trade for recall, and vice versa. The four resolution paths are:
edit the content, narrow allow entry plus ledger record, detector
ratchet with corpus measurement, or escalate to a security
reviewer.

% Source: bench/scripts/gate.sh; bench/cli/replay.py.
\paragraph{An open, reproducible artifact.} The corpus, detectors,
measurement scripts, regression gate, and per-sample replay tool
are open-source. A reviewer can clone the repository, run
\texttt{bench/scripts/gate.sh}, and obtain the per-detector
precision and recall in under ten minutes; the same reviewer can
re-run any single sample through \texttt{bench/cli/replay.py} and
see its deterministic verdict against the most recent baseline.

\subsection{Results in one paragraph}

% Source: docs/evaluation.md (auto-generated).
On the v0.2 corpus (Top 10 v0.1), macro
F\textsubscript{1} is 1.000 on the 99-sample in-sample slice and
1.000 on the 6-sample holdout slice. Per-detector precision and
recall are individually 1.000 across all ten detectors on the
in-sample slice and across the six detectors that have holdout
representation. Cross-language verdict-count parity is intact for
every detector on every sample. Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}
reports the per-detector breakdown, the methodology behind each
of these numbers, and an honest enumeration of the limitations
that make this a first measurement rather than a final one.

\subsection{Paper organization}

Section~\ref{sec:threat-model} introduces the Context CI Top 10
taxonomy in detail, with one subsection per category.
Section~\ref{sec:system-design} describes the three-pillar
architecture (Trust Contract, Top 10, Context Bill of Materials)
that the Top 10 sits inside. Section~\ref{sec:implementation}
covers the dual-implementation discipline, the regression gate,
and the FP/FN ledger methodology. Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}
reports per-detector P, R, and F\textsubscript{1}, the holdout
methodology, and the limitations.
Section~\ref{sec:related} positions PrimitiveHub against prior
work in software supply-chain security, prompt-injection defense,
and antivirus industry practice. Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}
closes with future work --- corpus expansion, multilingual coverage,
runtime gating, and the move to a generic Unicode-confusables
scope.
