\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Software supply-chain provenance}

A decade of supply-chain work targeted the build pipeline.
SLSA~\cite{SLSA:2023:provenance-spec} formalizes provenance levels;
in-toto~\cite{in-toto:2016:supply-chain} provides the predicate-based
attestation framework we inherit; Sigstore~\cite{Sigstore:2021:keyless-signing}
provides keyless signing for the public-registry path. All verify that a
compiled artifact came from a specific commit and was signed by an
authorized identity. PrimitiveHub adds a semantic-validation step
\emph{before} signing, so content the chain would otherwise certify as
authentic-but-unsafe is rejected at publish time. The incident record
that motivates the extension is well
cataloged~\cite{SolarWinds:2020:supply-chain,Codecov:2021:bash-uploader,ua-parser-js:2021:npm-typosquat,eslint-config-prettier:2024:maintainer-compromise,xz-utils:2024:CVE-2024-3094,Shai-Hulud:2025:npm-worm};
each exploited or motivated a layer of the SLSA/Sigstore/in-toto stack,
and the Top~10 names the analogous layer for agent context.

\subsection{Prompt-injection defense}

Prior prompt-injection work is largely runtime. Perez and
Ribeiro~\cite{Perez:2022:ignore-previous-prompt} first characterized
goal-hijacking and prompt-leaking attacks on deployed models. Greshake et
al.~\cite{Greshake:2023:prompt-injection-survey} catalog the indirect
injection surface and its runtime mitigations: input filtering,
retrieval sanitization, output post-processing. Wallace et
al.~\cite{Wallace:2024:instruction-hierarchy} train the model to rank
privileged against unprivileged instruction sources at inference time.
Both operate at execution time, and both leave part of a caught attack's
cost already paid. PrimitiveHub moves the check earlier: detectors run at
publish time, verification runs at load time, and the artifact reaches
the agent only after passing. The shift mirrors the package-manager move
from ``warn on install'' to ``block known-bad.'' The two lines are
complementary: a runtime defense catches what the static pass missed,
and the static pass keeps the runtime defense's workload smaller.

\subsection{OWASP Top 10 for LLM applications}

The OWASP Top~10 for LLM Applications~\cite{OWASP:2025:llm-top10} spans
prompt injection (LLM01), insecure output handling, training-data
poisoning, supply-chain risk (LLM05), and others. Our Top~10 overlaps it
at LLM01 and LLM05, the slice detectable statically on content; the
crosswalk documents the alignment row by row and disclaims the runtime
categories (insecure output handling, denial of service, post-inference
disclosure) as out of scope rather than claiming coverage.

\subsection{Antivirus methodology}

The antivirus field established rule-based malicious-pattern detection
and the practice of maintaining it as a
product~\cite{Christodorescu:2003:static-malware,Alvarez:2014:yara}.
PrimitiveHub adapts the operational scaffolding around that detection,
namely append-only false-positive and false-negative ledgers,
per-detector versioning, and a corpus that serves as ground truth, to the
agent-context substrate. What changes is the substrate: primitives are
markdown documents with semantic content, not binaries, so the detection
techniques diverge (regex pools with markdown-context awareness, skeleton
comparison for vendor names, codepoint-density checks) even as the
surrounding discipline stays AV-standard.

\subsection{Unicode-based attacks}

The confusables detector uses the UTS~\#39 skeleton
method~\cite{UTS39:2024:unicode-confusables}. The method is
standardized; applying it to vendor-name impersonation in agent context
is the new target. Homograph and confusable attacks have been documented
against internationalized domain names for over two
decades~\cite{Gabrilovich:2002:homograph}; we bring the same tradecraft
to the primitive registry, where the same attacker behavior appears in
field reports.

\subsection{Measurement methodology}

The precision/recall/F\textsubscript{1} convention follows
Powers~\cite{Powers:2011:evaluation-precision-recall}, with unweighted
macro-averaging because each category is a separate defense. The
held-out discipline follows the train/test tradition long standard in
machine learning~\cite{Kohavi:1995:cross-validation}, applied per
detector at an 80/20 ratio~\cite{primitivehub:2026:adr-0038}. The
per-sample replay surface has no direct analogue in classical AV work,
where signature databases were proprietary; the closer parallel is the
open conformance-suite tradition, where labeled inputs and per-test
outcomes are public so independent implementations can be compared. The
fake-citation detector consults two external authority taxonomies as
ground truth at runtime: the CVE catalog~\cite{MITRE:CVE:1999:taxonomy}
and the US patent issuance record~\cite{USPTO:1790:patent-system}. The
open held-out coverage gaps are tracked as future
work~\cite{primitivehub:2026:eval-limitations}.
